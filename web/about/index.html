About the Queensland Eye Institute

The Queensland Eye Institute combines specialist clinical care, Queensland's largest independent eye research institute and a commitment to education, all under the governance of the Queensland Eye Institute Foundation, a registered charity.

Our purpose

To make significant scientific, educational and clinical contributions to eye health, especially in Australia and its near neighbours.

Our vision

To be internationally recognised for our efforts in research, education and clinical care to reduce eye disease and promote eye health in the community.

Our mission

To provide excellence in research, education and clinical care to reduce eye disease, improve eye health and ultimately eliminate preventable blindness in the community.

Our values

Our values serve as a compass for our actions and describe how we behave in the world. We provide the mentorship, training and tools our students and researchers need to make a difference, and we develop education programmes that allow staff, students, registrars, fellows and eye care workers to reach their full potential. We aim to create a working environment in which trust, integrity, collaboration, cooperation, personal initiative and responsibility thrive. We learn by experience. We lead by example.

One institute, three pillars

  • Clinical care. The QEI Clinic at Woolloongabba and Clayfield treats more than 16,000 patients a year. Our ophthalmologists are all Fellows of the Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Ophthalmologists with subspecialty fellowship training, supported by an optometrist-led Dry Eye Clinic, the South Brisbane Day Hospital and the Queensland Electro-Diagnostic and Imaging Centre.
  • Research. The QEI Foundation operates Queensland's largest independent academic research institute devoted to eye-related health and diseases, with laboratories, a clinical trials unit and collaborations across Australia and the world. Explore our research.
  • Education. We are a clinical school of the University of Queensland Medical School and train registrars, fellows, optometry and medical students, optometrists and practice staff. See our education programmes.

Governance and accountability

The Queensland Eye Institute is operated by the Queensland Eye Institute Foundation (QEIF), a not-for-profit organisation registered with the Australian Charities and Not-for-profits Commission and endorsed as a Deductible Gift Recipient. QEIF is governed by a Board of Directors with health, medical, business, finance and philanthropic experience and led by an Executive Director and Chief Executive Officer. With no recurrent government funding, the institute relies on the generosity of donors, businesses and the community, and all profits from the South Brisbane Day Hospital are reinvested in the Foundation's work.

The South Brisbane Day Hospital is accredited to the National Safety and Quality Health Service Standards. Our privacy practices comply with the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, and patients' rights are protected under the Australian Charter of Healthcare Rights. Read our privacy policy and your rights.

Our history

  • 1965

    The Australian Foundation for the Prevention of Blindness (Queensland Division) is incorporated, with Dr John Ohlrich the driving force.

  • 1986

    The University of Queensland Chair of Ophthalmology is established at the Princess Alexandra Hospital with the support of Charles Viertel OBE, OPSM, the Royal Australian College of Ophthalmologists, Lions International and Perpetual Trustees. Professor Lawrence Hirst is appointed inaugural Chair.

  • 1991

    The Foundation becomes the Prevent Blindness Foundation (PBF).

  • 2005

    The Queensland Eye Institute officially opens at the Mater Hospital with Professor Lawrence Hirst as its first CEO.

  • 2010

    Professor Mark Radford is appointed CEO of PBF and QEI.

  • 2013

    The Sylvia and Charles Viertel Charitable Foundation grant enables the redevelopment of 140 Melbourne Street, South Brisbane and the establishment of South Bank Day Hospital. PBF becomes the Queensland Eye Institute Foundation.

  • 2014

    The QEI Clinic opens in South Brisbane and QEIF acquires the Visual Electro-Diagnostic and Imaging Centre, now the Queensland Electro-Diagnostic and Imaging Centre.

  • 2019

    QEI expands, welcoming patients to an additional clinic in Clayfield.

  • 2024

    QEI relocates all operations, including clinical services, the research laboratory, the clinical trials team and QEDIC, to 87 Ipswich Road, Woolloongabba.

Acknowledgement of Country

We acknowledge the Turrbal and Yugara peoples as the traditional owners of the lands where QEI now stands. We pay respect to their Elders, past, current and emerging. We recognise that these lands have always been places of teaching, research, learning and healing.
