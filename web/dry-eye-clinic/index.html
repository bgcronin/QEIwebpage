Dry eye deserves a dedicated clinic

Dry eye disease affects a large proportion of Australians and is one of the most common reasons people visit an eye care professional. It can make reading, driving, screen work and even a walk outside miserable, and it rarely resolves on its own.

The good news is that dry eye is highly treatable when the underlying cause is identified. Most cases are caused by meibomian gland dysfunction, where the oil glands in the eyelids become blocked or inflamed so the tears evaporate too quickly. Others are caused by reduced tear production, eyelid inflammation (blepharitis), Demodex mites, allergy, medications, hormonal changes or environmental factors, and often several factors at once.

Our Dry Eye Clinic brings together the assessment tools, in-clinic treatments and prescribing expertise needed to treat each of those causes, in a service that is quick and easy to access.

What sets the QEI Dry Eye Clinic apart

  • Led by optometrists, so you are seen quickly. No referral, no waiting for a specialist list.
  • One of the best equipped dry eye clinics in Australia, with the Lumenis OptiLight IPL, Rexon-Eye QMR, the Eye-light low level light therapy mask and BlephEx on site.
  • Hands-on procedures including meibomian gland expression, punctal plug insertion and Demodex treatment.
  • Prescribing expertise for anti-inflammatory therapies such as cyclosporine eye drops when they are needed.
  • Part of the Queensland Eye Institute, so if your eyes need an ophthalmologist you are already in the right place.

Is the Dry Eye Clinic right for you?

Book an assessment if you regularly notice:

  • Gritty, burning, stinging or scratchy eyes
  • Eyes that water, especially in wind or air-conditioning
  • Vision that blurs and clears when you blink
  • Tired, heavy eyes after screens or reading
  • Red or crusty eyelids, or recurrent styes
  • Contact lenses that feel uncomfortable
  • Symptoms that persist despite lubricating drops

Sudden vision loss, severe pain or a red eye after injury or contact lens wear needs same-day care: see emergency eye care.

Technology and treatments

One of the best equipped dry eye clinics in Australia

Treatment is matched to the cause of your dry eye. Most people benefit from a combination of in-clinic procedures and a simple home routine.

Meibomian gland dysfunction

IPL (OptiLight)

OptiLight is the first intense pulsed light (IPL) device approved by the US Food and Drug Administration for the management of dry eye disease due to meibomian gland dysfunction. Precisely controlled light pulses reduce inflammation around the eyes and help the eyelid oil glands work properly again.

How it works
All types of dry eye

QMR (Rexon-Eye)

Rexon-Eye uses quantum molecular resonance (QMR), a gentle, non-invasive electrical stimulation that encourages the tear and oil glands to regenerate. It is one of the few treatments that helps both evaporative and aqueous-deficient dry eye.

How it works
Meibomian gland dysfunction

Low level light therapy

Low level light therapy (LLLT), also called photobiomodulation, uses a mask of gentle red LED light to warm and energise the eyelid glands. It is painless, suits every skin type and is often paired with IPL.

How it works
Blepharitis

BlephEx

BlephEx is an in-clinic procedure that precisely cleans the eyelid margins and lash bases, removing the biofilm, debris and mite residue that drive blepharitis and dry eye. It achieves what lid wipes at home cannot.

How it works
Meibomian gland dysfunction

Meibomian gland expression

When the oil glands in the eyelids are blocked, warming and gently expressing them clears the thickened oil so a healthy tear film can form again. Our optometrists are highly experienced in this hands-on procedure.

How it works
Aqueous-deficient dry eye

Punctal plugs

Punctal plugs are tiny, painless inserts placed in the tear drainage ducts so your natural tears stay on the surface of the eye for longer. They are a simple, reversible option when your eyes do not make enough tears.

How it works
Demodex blepharitis

Demodex treatment

Demodex are microscopic mites that live at the base of the eyelashes. In large numbers they cause itchy, crusty eyelids, recurrent styes and chronic dry eye. Our optometrists diagnose Demodex under magnification and treat it with targeted in-clinic and home therapies.

How it works
Inflammatory dry eye

Prescription therapy

Inflammation is both a cause and a consequence of dry eye. Our therapeutically endorsed optometrists can prescribe anti-inflammatory treatments such as cyclosporine eye drops, and coordinate other medicines with your GP or QEI ophthalmologist when needed.

Your first visit

What to expect

  1. Book your assessment

    Call 07 3239 5000 and ask for the Dry Eye Clinic. No referral is needed. If you have one, bring it along with any drops you use.

  2. A thorough dry eye work-up

    Your optometrist reviews your history and examines your tear film, eyelids and meibomian glands to identify what is driving your symptoms.

  3. Your personal treatment plan

    We explain the findings, recommend in-clinic treatments where they will help, and set up a home routine. Costs are explained before anything is booked.

  4. Treatment and review

    Most in-clinic treatments are a short course of sessions a few weeks apart, followed by a review to measure your progress and fine-tune the plan.

More about your appointment

Common questions

Do I need a referral to attend the Dry Eye Clinic?

No. Because the clinic is led by optometrists you can book directly by calling 07 3239 5000. GPs, optometrists and ophthalmologists are also welcome to refer patients to the clinic.

How soon can I be seen?

Appointments are readily available, without the long waits that are often needed to see an ophthalmologist. Call us and we will offer you the next suitable time.

Will Medicare or my health fund cover treatment?

Medicare rebates may apply to optometry consultations. In-clinic procedures such as IPL, QMR, light therapy and BlephEx are generally not covered by Medicare, although some private health extras cover part of the cost. We provide a written quote before you commit to a treatment course.

What should I bring?

Any eye drops, ointments or supplements you use, a list of your medications, your glasses or contact lenses, and your Medicare and health fund cards. Please avoid eye make-up on the day of any lid procedure.
