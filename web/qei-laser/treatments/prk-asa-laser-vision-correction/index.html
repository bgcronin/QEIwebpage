QEI Laser treatment

PRK and ASA laser vision correction

Photorefractive keratectomy (PRK) and advanced surface ablation (ASA) reshape the surface of the cornea with the excimer laser to correct short-sightedness, long-sightedness and astigmatism, reducing or removing the need for glasses and contact lenses. No corneal flap is created, which makes surface laser a safe option for many people who are not suited to LASIK.

At a glance

Best for
Adults with a stable prescription who want less dependence on glasses or contact lenses, including people with thinner corneas or active lifestyles who are not ideal LASIK candidates
Procedure
The surface layer is removed and the excimer laser reshapes the cornea; a bandage contact lens is placed
Anaesthetic
Eye drops; day procedure at South Brisbane Day Hospital
Recovery
Discomfort for 3 to 5 days; functional vision within about a week, stabilising over 1 to 3 months
Health fund
Refractive procedure: not automatically covered by health funds or Medicare. Some funds may contribute to part of the fee; a written quote is provided

How surface laser works

The cornea provides most of the eye's focusing power, so changing its shape by a few micrometres changes your prescription. In PRK and ASA the thin surface layer of cells (the epithelium) is gently removed, the excimer laser reshapes the cornea in seconds according to your prescription and corneal map, and a bandage contact lens is placed while the surface regrows over the next few days. Because no flap is cut, the structural strength of the cornea is preserved.

Where refractive surgery happens

QEI's laser surgeons perform refractive procedures, including LASIK, TransPRK, PRK, ASA, lenticule extraction (CLEAR/KLEx), implantable collamer lenses and refractive lens exchange, through Focus Vision Clinic, which they co-founded and which is co-located with QEI at 87 Ipswich Road, Woolloongabba. All surgery is performed at South Brisbane Day Hospital.

Who chooses surface laser?

Surface laser is often recommended for people with thinner corneas, people whose corneal shape makes a flap inadvisable, those in contact sports or occupations with a risk of eye injury, and those with a tendency to dry eye. It is also used to fine-tune vision after previous surgery. For people who want the fastest recovery and are good candidates, LASIK may be preferred; for higher prescriptions, implantable lenses or lens replacement may be better. Your surgeon will explain the options after a thorough assessment.

What to expect

  • Assessment. Contact lenses are left out for a period before your assessment so the cornea returns to its natural shape. Your surgeon maps and measures your cornea, checks your prescription and eye health, and discusses realistic outcomes and costs.
  • The procedure. Both eyes are usually treated in the same session, under anaesthetic drops, taking around ten to fifteen minutes in total.
  • Recovery. Expect discomfort, watering and light sensitivity for three to five days until the bandage lens is removed. Vision is blurry at first, becomes functional within about a week and continues to sharpen over one to three months. Drops are used for several weeks and follow-up visits check healing.

Risks

Surface laser is a well-established procedure with an excellent safety record, but no surgery is without risk. Possible issues include infection, delayed healing, corneal haze, dry eye, glare or haloes at night, and under- or over-correction that may need a further treatment. These are discussed in detail during informed consent. See information for patients.

Questions about PRK and ASA

How is PRK different from LASIK and SMILE?

All three reshape the cornea with a laser. LASIK creates a thin flap and treats the tissue beneath it, giving fast visual recovery. SMILE removes a small lenticule of tissue through a keyhole incision. PRK and ASA treat the surface directly with no flap or incision, so there is no flap-related risk and more corneal strength is preserved, at the cost of a slower, more uncomfortable recovery. QEI's refractive surgeons offer LASIK as well as surface laser and lens-based procedures, and will recommend the option best suited to your eyes.

Am I suitable for laser vision correction?

Suitability depends on your age, the stability of your prescription, the thickness and shape of your cornea, the health of the surface of your eye and your general health. Keratoconus, significant dry eye, some autoimmune conditions and pregnancy are among the reasons laser may be delayed or not recommended. A detailed assessment including corneal topography and tomography decides this.

Is it covered by my health fund?

Health funds will not automatically pay for solely refractive procedures such as PRK and ASA. Some funds, such as BUPA Ultimate and Defence Health, may cover some of the fee, but this is not guaranteed or automatic. Costs are explained in a written quote at your assessment.
