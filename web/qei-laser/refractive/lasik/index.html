QEI Laser Refractive

LASIK eye surgery in Brisbane

LASIK (laser-assisted in situ keratomileusis) is the world's most performed vision correction procedure. A femtosecond laser creates a thin flap in the cornea, an excimer laser reshapes the tissue beneath it, and the flap is replaced. Most people see well the next morning. QEI Laser Refractive offers Ray Tracing LASIK, personalised from a 3D model of your whole eye.

At a glance

Short-sightedness, long-sightedness and astigmatism; typically up to about −10 D of short-sightedness, +4 D of long-sightedness and 5 D of astigmatism, subject to corneal thickness

Recovery

What is LASIK?

LASIK reshapes the cornea, the clear window at the front of the eye, so that light focuses precisely on the retina without glasses or contact lenses. It corrects short-sightedness (myopia) by flattening the central cornea, long-sightedness (hyperopia) by steepening it, and astigmatism by making it more evenly curved. The reshaping takes seconds and the change is permanent.

Two lasers are used. A femtosecond laser creates a thin, hinged flap in the surface of the cornea in about 20 seconds. The flap is folded back and an excimer laser removes a precise, computer-controlled layer of tissue from the cornea beneath it, a few thousandths of a millimetre at a time. The flap is then laid back into place, where it bonds naturally within minutes and heals without stitches. Because the surface layer of the cornea is preserved, LASIK has the fastest and most comfortable recovery of any laser procedure.

Ray Tracing LASIK: a treatment designed for your eye alone

At QEI Laser Refractive, LASIK is planned with WaveLight Plus ray-tracing technology on the Alcon WaveLight EX500 excimer laser. The InnovEyes Sightmap measures the shape of the front and back of your cornea, the aberrations of your whole optical system, the position of your natural lens and the length of your eye, and combines them into a three-dimensional model of that eye. The treatment is then calculated by tracing light through the model, rather than from your glasses prescription alone. The result is a more precise correction with the potential for sharper-than-20/20 vision and fewer night-vision symptoms. Our surgeons were the first in Queensland to offer this technology.

Who is LASIK for?

LASIK suits most adults with a stable prescription and a healthy cornea that is thick enough to leave a safe margin of tissue after treatment. Typical criteria include:

  • age 18 or over, and ideally over 21, with a prescription that has not changed significantly for 12 months;
  • short-sightedness up to about −10 D, long-sightedness up to about +4 D and astigmatism up to about 5 D, depending on your corneal thickness;
  • normal corneal shape on tomography (no keratoconus or suspicious thinning), leaving at least 250 to 300 micrometres of untouched cornea after treatment and altering no more than about 40 per cent of the corneal thickness;
  • a healthy ocular surface without severe dry eye, and no uncontrolled autoimmune disease, pregnancy or breastfeeding.

If your cornea is thin, your prescription is high, or you have dry eye, one of the other procedures may be safer or more accurate. See am I suitable? and compare the procedures.

What happens on the day

  1. You arrive at South Brisbane Day Hospital, in the same building as the QEI Clinic, having eaten normally. Sedation from our on-site anaesthetist is available if you are anxious.
  2. Anaesthetic drops numb the eyes. A gentle suction ring holds the eye still while the femtosecond laser creates the flap; you feel pressure and your vision dims for about 20 seconds.
  3. Under the excimer laser you look at a fixation light while the treatment is delivered, typically 10 to 30 seconds per eye. An eye tracker follows any small movements.
  4. The flap is repositioned, drops are applied, and both eyes are treated in one visit. You are in theatre for about 20 minutes.
  5. You rest at home with your eyes closed for a few hours. Someone must drive you.

Recovery

Vision is hazy and the eyes feel gritty and watery for the first few hours. By the next morning most people see well enough to drive and are back at work within one to two days. Antibiotic and anti-inflammatory drops are used for one to two weeks, lubricating drops for several months. Avoid rubbing your eyes, eye make-up for a week, swimming for two weeks and contact sports for a month. Vision continues to sharpen over the first few weeks and is stable by three months.

Results and risks

Large studies report that more than 95 per cent of LASIK patients are satisfied and that the great majority achieve driving-standard vision or better without glasses. Risks are low but real, and we discuss each of them with you during informed consent:

  • Dry eye, common in the first months and usually settling by three to six months.
  • Night glare and halos, common early and usually fading; less frequent with ray-traced treatments.
  • Under- or over-correction needing an enhancement in about 2 to 5 per cent of eyes.
  • Flap complications such as wrinkles, debris or inflammation beneath the flap, uncommon and usually treatable.
  • Infection, rare (in the order of 1 in several thousand) and treated with drops.
  • Corneal ectasia, a progressive weakening of the cornea, historically about 1 in 2,000 and now rarer with tomographic screening, which is why we never operate on a cornea that shows early keratoconus.

  • Flaps created by the Ziemer FEMTO LDV Z8, the lowest-energy femtosecond laser available.

  • Corneal subspecialist surgeons who treat the whole spectrum of corneal disease, not only refractive surgery.
  • One all-inclusive price covering assessment, surgery and a full year of aftercare, including enhancement if needed. See costs and payment.

Questions about LASIK

What is Ray Tracing LASIK and is it better?

Conventional LASIK plans the treatment from your glasses prescription and a corneal map. Ray Tracing LASIK (Alcon WaveLight Plus) builds a digital model of your entire optical system, including the front and back surfaces of the cornea, the position of the lens and the length of the eye, from measurements taken by the InnovEyes Sightmap, and calculates the exact corneal shape that will focus light most precisely for that eye. In the pivotal study, 94.4 per cent of eyes achieved 20/20 or better and 70.2 per cent achieved 20/16 or better without glasses. Our surgeons were the first in Queensland to offer it.

Is LASIK safe?

LASIK has been performed on tens of millions of eyes over more than 30 years and is one of the most studied elective procedures in medicine. In the large PROWL studies run with the United States Food and Drug Administration, more than 95 per cent of patients were satisfied with their result and visual symptoms such as glare and halos were more often improved than worsened. Serious complications are rare, and the most important safeguard is careful screening to exclude eyes that are not suitable, which is why we use corneal tomography and see every patient with the surgeon before agreeing to operate.

Will I get dry eye?

Almost everyone has some dryness in the first weeks after LASIK because the corneal nerves that trigger tear production are cut when the flap is made. For most people this settles within three to six months with lubricating drops. People who already have significant dry eye may be better suited to CLEAR, TransPRK or the ICL, and our optometrist-led Dry Eye Clinic can treat the tear film before and after surgery.

Can the flap move?

The flap seals quickly and, once healed, is very stable. The risk of it dislodging is highest in the first days, which is why we ask you not to rub your eyes and to wear shields at night for the first week. Contact sports and swimming should wait a month. People at ongoing risk of eye trauma may prefer a flap-free option such as CLEAR or TransPRK.

What if my vision is not perfect afterwards?

Around 2 to 5 per cent of eyes retain a small prescription that is worth treating. An enhancement, usually by lifting the flap and applying a small additional laser treatment, can be performed once the eye has stabilised, typically after three months. Enhancements within the first year are included in our aftercare.

This information is general in nature and does not replace advice from your own doctor or optometrist. If you are worried about your eyes, please call us on 07 3239 5000.
