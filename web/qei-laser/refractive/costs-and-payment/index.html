QEI Laser Refractive

Laser eye surgery cost in Brisbane: prices, payment plans and rebates

Transparent, all-inclusive pricing for LASIK, CLEAR, TransPRK, PRK, the EVO ICL and refractive lens exchange at QEI Laser Refractive, with interest-free payment plans, and a plain explanation of what Medicare, private health funds and the tax system do and do not contribute.

LASIK and Ray Tracing LASIK From $4,250 As above

Refractive lens exchange From $6,000 As above, plus anaesthetist and a premium (trifocal, extended-depth-of-focus or toric) lens

'From' prices reflect the lowest complexity; higher prescriptions, astigmatism and custom lenses may sit above the starting figure. Every patient receives a written quote after their assessment, and that figure does not change. Prices are reviewed annually.

What is included

  • The laser vision assessment fee, which is credited against your procedure.
  • Your surgeon's fee and the hospital fee at South Brisbane Day Hospital.
  • The anaesthetist for ICL and lens exchange, and sedation if needed for laser.
  • All routine post-operative reviews for 12 months.
  • An enhancement (touch-up) treatment within the first 12 months if your surgeon considers it appropriate.
  • A written report to your optometrist and GP.

What is not included

  • Prescription eye drops and medicines, which you collect from a pharmacy (typically $50 to $120).
  • Treatment of pre-existing dry eye through the Dry Eye Clinic, which is billed separately and attracts Medicare rebates where applicable.
  • Unrelated eye care and any surgery beyond the first 12 months.

Medicare, health funds and tax

Medicare provides no rebate for vision correction surgery on a healthy eye, but it does contribute to your assessment consultation and tests when you attend with a referral. Where the operation treats disease, for example cataract surgery or therapeutic laser for corneal scarring, Medicare and hospital cover apply; see QEI Laser Therapeutic.

Private health funds vary. A minority of funds contribute a fixed amount towards laser eye surgery from their highest extras tiers, often with a waiting period of one to three years and a lifetime limit; most policies contribute nothing. Check with your fund before your assessment and ask for the exact benefit in writing.

Tax and superannuation. Vision correction is a private expense: it is not tax deductible and early release of superannuation is generally not available for it.

Payment plans

Interest-free plans over up to 24 months are available through our finance partner, subject to approval, and can be arranged after your assessment. You may also pay by card, bank transfer or a combination. A deposit secures your surgery date and the balance is due before surgery.

Is it worth it?

The average contact lens wearer spends between $700 and $1,200 a year on lenses and solutions, and a spectacle wearer several hundred dollars a year on frames and lenses, over decades. For most people laser vision correction repays its cost within five to eight years, and the ICL or lens exchange within ten. The value that matters more to most of our patients is practical: waking to a clear room, sport without lenses, travel without supplies, and, for lens exchange patients, the end of reading glasses.

Frequently asked questions

Why do prices vary so much between clinics?

Some advertised prices exclude the assessment, the hospital or day-surgery fee, the anaesthetist, follow-up visits or enhancement, and some are 'from' prices that apply only to the lowest prescriptions on the oldest technology. Our prices include everything except prescription medicines, and the figure quoted to you in writing after your assessment is the figure you pay.

Is the assessment free?

No. A laser vision assessment is a full specialist examination with the surgeon who would treat you, and it carries a fee, which is deducted from the cost of your procedure if you go ahead. We do not offer free screenings because the assessment is the most important part of the process and deserves the time and the equipment of a proper consultation.

Does Medicare cover laser eye surgery?

No. Medicare classifies vision correction of a healthy eye as elective and provides no rebate for the surgery. Medicare does contribute to the assessment consultation and tests when you have a referral from your GP or optometrist, and it covers surgery that treats disease, such as cataract surgery or therapeutic laser for corneal conditions, which is provided through QEI Laser Therapeutic.

Will my health fund pay?

Possibly in part. A small number of funds include laser eye surgery in their top-tier extras policies, typically paying a fixed amount per eye or a lifetime limit after a waiting period of one to three years. Most policies contribute nothing. Because the surgery is not performed under a Medicare item number, hospital cover does not apply. Call your fund, quote the procedure you are considering and ask specifically about 'laser eye surgery' or 'refractive surgery' under extras. We provide itemised receipts for any claim.

Is laser eye surgery tax deductible?

Not for most people. The net medical expenses tax offset was phased out in 2019, and elective surgery is a private expense. Early release of superannuation on compassionate grounds is generally not approved for vision correction. If you hold a specific occupational reason, seek your own advice from an accountant.

How does the payment plan work?

After your assessment you can apply for an interest-free plan that spreads the cost over up to 24 months, subject to approval by the finance provider. A small establishment fee may apply. Our team will give you the details and the application takes minutes.
