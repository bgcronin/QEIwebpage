QEI Laser Refractive

PRK and ASA laser eye surgery in Brisbane

Photorefractive keratectomy (PRK), also called advanced surface ablation (ASA), was the first laser vision correction procedure and remains one of the safest. The surface layer of the cornea is gently removed, the excimer laser reshapes the tissue beneath, and the surface regrows within days. QEI Laser Refractive offers PRK alongside its no-touch successor, TransPRK.

At a glance

Corrects
Short-sightedness, long-sightedness and astigmatism, in a similar range to LASIK
How
The epithelium is removed with a dilute alcohol solution or soft brush, then the excimer laser reshapes the cornea
Procedure time
About 5 to 10 minutes per eye
Recovery
Surface heals in about 4 to 5 days under a bandage contact lens; driving usually within 1 to 2 weeks; final vision by 1 to 3 months
Best for
Thinner corneas, eyes with surface irregularity, treatments combined with therapeutic laser, and people who prefer a flap-free procedure

What is PRK?

Photorefractive keratectomy was the first laser vision correction procedure, introduced in the late 1980s, and its long-term results are the best documented of any technique. The surface layer of the cornea (the epithelium) is removed, the excimer laser reshapes the corneal tissue beneath it to correct your prescription, and the epithelium regrows over the new surface within days. The terms PRK, advanced surface ablation (ASA) and surface laser describe the same family of procedures.

In conventional PRK the surgeon removes the epithelium with a dilute alcohol solution or a soft rotating brush. In TransPRK, the laser removes the epithelium itself in a no-touch, single-step treatment. Both are performed at QEI Laser Refractive, and your surgeon will recommend the technique that suits your cornea.

Advantages of surface laser

  • No flap. The deeper cornea is untouched, leaving maximum strength and the lowest risk of ectasia of any laser procedure.
  • Suitable for thinner corneas and for people with a lifestyle or occupation that carries a risk of eye injury.
  • Lower risk of long-term dry eye than LASIK, because fewer corneal nerves are cut.
  • The same visual results as LASIK once healing is complete.

The trade-off

Surface laser has a slower recovery than LASIK or CLEAR. The eye is uncomfortable for three to five days while the surface heals under a bandage contact lens, vision is blurry for the first week and continues to sharpen for one to three months, and drops are used for several weeks. Mitomycin C is applied during surgery for higher corrections to reduce the risk of haze.

What happens on the day

  1. Anaesthetic drops numb the eye and a small speculum keeps the eyelids open.
  2. The epithelium is loosened and removed from the central cornea.
  3. You look at a fixation light while the excimer laser reshapes the cornea, typically 30 to 60 seconds per eye.
  4. A bandage contact lens and drops are applied. Both eyes are usually treated at the same visit, and someone drives you home.

Results and risks

PRK's risks are those of surface laser generally: delayed epithelial healing, temporary haze, early glare and halos, under- or over-correction that may need an enhancement, and rare infection in the first days. Serious complications are very uncommon. See compare the procedures, am I suitable? and costs and payment.

PRK in therapeutic surgery

The same surface technique underpins many of the procedures in QEI Laser Therapeutic, where topography-guided ablation regularises irregular corneas, removes scars and dystrophies and treats recurrent erosions. Patients whose refractive treatment is combined with a therapeutic procedure may be eligible for Medicare and health fund contributions; your surgeon will explain what applies to you.

Questions about PRK

Should I have PRK or TransPRK?

For most people TransPRK is the better version of the same operation: it heals about a day faster with less early discomfort and haze, because nothing touches the eye. Conventional PRK remains useful when the epithelium needs to be removed by hand, for example when the surface is irregular, when a previous procedure is being refined, or when the laser treatment is combined with a therapeutic procedure. Your surgeon will recommend the technique that suits your eye.

Is PRK less accurate than LASIK?

No. Long-term studies show the same visual results from PRK and LASIK. The difference is the recovery: PRK is slower and less comfortable in the first week, but it leaves a stronger cornea and has a lower risk of dry eye and no flap-related risks.

Can PRK be repeated?

Yes. A small residual prescription can be treated with a further surface treatment once the eye has stabilised, usually after three to six months. Your surgeon will discuss whether an enhancement is appropriate for you and what it involves.
