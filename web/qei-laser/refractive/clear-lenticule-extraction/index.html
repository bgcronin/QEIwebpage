QEI Laser Refractive

CLEAR lenticule extraction: keyhole laser eye surgery in Brisbane

CLEAR (Corneal Lenticule Extraction for Advanced Refractive correction) is keyhole, flap-free laser vision correction for short-sightedness and astigmatism. A femtosecond laser shapes a thin disc of tissue, called a lenticule, inside the cornea, and the surgeon removes it through an opening only a few millimetres wide. At QEI Laser Refractive, CLEAR is performed on the Ziemer FEMTO LDV Z8, the lowest-energy femtosecond laser available, which means a gentler treatment and a smoother, faster recovery.

At a glance

Corrects
Short-sightedness from about −0.50 to −10.00 D, with up to 5 D of astigmatism (a combined prescription of up to about −12.50 D). Long-sightedness cannot be treated with CLEAR
How
The Ziemer FEMTO LDV Z8 femtosecond laser cuts a thin lenticule inside the cornea, which the surgeon removes through a 2 to 4 mm keyhole incision. No flap is made and no excimer laser is used
Procedure time
About 10 to 15 minutes per eye; the laser itself takes well under a minute. Both eyes are treated in one visit
Recovery
Comfortable within hours; good vision within 1 to 3 days, sharpening over the following weeks; most people drive and work within a few days
Best for
Short-sighted adults who want a flap-free procedure: contact sports and physical occupations, eyes prone to dryness, and anyone who values keeping the cornea as strong as possible
Guide price (2026)
From $4,250 per eye, all-inclusive, with interest-free payment plans

What is CLEAR lenticule extraction?

CLEAR corrects short-sightedness and astigmatism by removing a precisely shaped, contact-lens-shaped disc of tissue, the lenticule, from inside the cornea. The femtosecond laser makes two curved cuts a fraction of a millimetre apart, defining the top and bottom of the lenticule, and a short side cut at the edge of the cornea. The surgeon gently separates the lenticule and lifts it out through the side cut. With the lenticule gone the cornea flattens by exactly the amount needed to bring distant objects into focus.

The whole treatment happens beneath an intact surface. There is no flap, no excimer laser and no tissue removed from the surface of the eye. Only a small keyhole incision of 2 to 4 millimetres, which heals within a day, separates the lenticule from the outside world.

Why the Ziemer Z8 matters

Lenticule extraction depends on the femtosecond laser producing cuts so smooth that the lenticule separates cleanly and the tissue planes above and below it heal invisibly. The Ziemer FEMTO LDV Z8 does this differently from other lenticule lasers:

  • Very low pulse energy. The Z8 fires millions of tiny, overlapping pulses of only 2 to 4 nanojoules each, a small fraction of the energy of other lenticule lasers. Lower energy means less tissue disruption, less inflammation and less light sensitivity afterwards, and cut surfaces that are exceptionally smooth.
  • Built-in OCT imaging. The laser images the cornea in cross-section during the procedure, so your surgeon can confirm the thickness and centration of the lenticule before it is created.
  • Gentle docking. The Z8 rests on the eye with a low-pressure interface, so the rise in eye pressure during the treatment is small and the red spots (subconjunctival haemorrhages) that older lasers commonly leave are less frequent.
  • One laser, many uses. Our surgeons use the same Z8 every week for LASIK flaps, for the tunnels in CAIRS keratoconus surgery and for laser-assisted lens surgery. It is a tool they know intimately.

How CLEAR compares with LASIK and TransPRK

Compared with LASIK, CLEAR gives up a little speed of visual recovery in exchange for structural advantages: no flap, more of the strong anterior cornea preserved, fewer corneal nerves cut and therefore less early dry eye. Comparative studies and meta-analyses consistently show a better tear film and quicker return of corneal sensation after lenticule extraction than after LASIK, with equal accuracy by three to six months.

Compared with TransPRK, CLEAR is far more comfortable and recovers much faster, because the surface of the cornea is untouched. TransPRK remains the better option for the thinnest corneas, for very low prescriptions and for eyes with surface irregularity, because it removes tissue from the front of the cornea rather than from within it.

Who is CLEAR for?

CLEAR suits most short-sighted adults, and it is our usual recommendation for people who want a flap-free procedure without the recovery time of surface laser. Typical criteria include:

  • age 18 or over, and ideally over 21, with a prescription stable for 12 months;
  • short-sightedness between about −0.50 and −10.00 D, with astigmatism up to 5 D;
  • a cornea thick enough to leave a healthy margin after the lenticule is removed (we plan a cap of about 110 to 130 micrometres and never leave less than 250 to 300 micrometres of untouched cornea);
  • normal corneal shape on tomography and a healthy ocular surface.

Long-sighted eyes, prescriptions beyond −10 D and eyes with early keratoconus are not suitable for CLEAR. The ICL is usually the best alternative for very high prescriptions and thin corneas.

What happens on the day

  1. You arrive at South Brisbane Day Hospital, in the same building as the QEI Clinic. Sedation from our anaesthetist is available if you are anxious.
  2. Anaesthetic drops numb the eye. The Z8 handpiece is docked gently on the eye and you look at a fixation light. The laser creates the lenticule and side cut in about 30 seconds; you feel light pressure and your vision dims briefly.
  3. Under the microscope your surgeon separates the lenticule with a fine instrument and removes it through the side cut. This takes a few minutes and you feel nothing but slight pressure.
  4. Drops are applied and the second eye is treated the same way. You are in theatre for about 20 to 30 minutes.
  5. You go home to rest with your eyes closed for a few hours. Someone must drive you.

Recovery

The eyes feel gritty and watery for a few hours and vision is misty on the first day. Most people are comfortable by the evening and see well enough to drive within two to four days. Antibiotic and anti-inflammatory drops are used for one to two weeks and lubricating drops for several weeks. There is no flap to protect, so you can return to the gym within a few days and to swimming and contact sports after two weeks. Vision continues to sharpen over the first month and is stable at three months.

Results and risks

In the published one-year study of CLEAR on the Z8, 95.6 per cent of eyes were within 0.50 D of the intended correction and 100 per cent within 1.00 D, results equivalent to modern LASIK. Risks are low and are discussed in full at your consultation:

  • Under- or over-correction needing an enhancement in a small percentage of eyes; enhancements within the first year are included.
  • Slower early recovery than LASIK, with misty vision for the first days.
  • Interface haze or inflammation, usually mild and treated with drops.
  • Difficulty removing the lenticule or a small tear at the edge of the cap, rare in experienced hands and usually without effect on the result.
  • Suction loss during the laser, uncommon; the treatment can usually be re-docked and completed the same day.
  • Infection, rare, and corneal ectasia, very rare with tomographic screening.
  • Dry eye, milder and shorter-lived than after LASIK, but still possible.

Questions about CLEAR lenticule extraction

Is CLEAR the same as SMILE?

They are the same family of procedure. Lenticule extraction was introduced under the brand name SMILE on the Zeiss VisuMax laser; CLEAR is the Ziemer version of the same idea, performed on the FEMTO LDV Z8, and the surgical community now uses the neutral term keratorefractive lenticule extraction (KLEx) for all of them. The Z8 differs in using pulses of only 2 to 4 nanojoules, a small fraction of the energy of other lenticule lasers, and in having optical coherence tomography (OCT) built into the laser so the surgeon can confirm the depth and position of the lenticule while it is being made. Both approaches remove a lenticule through a small opening rather than lifting a flap.

Why choose CLEAR instead of LASIK?

Because there is no flap. LASIK cuts a large hinged flap across the front of the cornea, which severs more corneal nerves and leaves a permanent interface. CLEAR reaches the treatment zone through a keyhole incision of a few millimetres, so more of the strong front layers of the cornea are preserved, there is no flap to dislodge in a knock to the eye, and the corneal nerves that keep the eye moist recover sooner. A 2025 meta-analysis of 18 comparative studies found that lenticule extraction patients had better tear film stability and corneal sensitivity than LASIK patients in the months after surgery. The trade-off is that vision takes a little longer to sharpen after CLEAR (days rather than hours) and long-sightedness cannot be treated.

Can CLEAR treat long-sightedness or reading vision?

Not at present. CLEAR is approved for short-sightedness and astigmatism. Long-sighted people are usually better suited to LASIK, TransPRK, the ICL or, from the mid-40s, refractive lens exchange. Reading vision (presbyopia) can be addressed with a monovision plan in CLEAR, where one eye is set for distance and the other slightly nearer, which we can trial with contact lenses beforehand.

How quickly will I see clearly after CLEAR?

Vision is misty for the first day, usually good enough to move around the house by the evening, and most people are legal to drive within two to four days. Because the lenticule is removed through a small opening, the tissue planes take a few days to settle, so the crispness you get overnight with LASIK arrives over the first one to two weeks with CLEAR. Final vision is reached at about three months.

What happens if I need a touch-up?

Enhancements after CLEAR are uncommon (in the order of 2 to 4 per cent of eyes). When they are needed, the Z8 allows a second, flapless lenticule treatment in suitable eyes, or a small surface laser treatment (TransPRK) can be applied over the cap. Enhancements in the first year are included in our aftercare.
