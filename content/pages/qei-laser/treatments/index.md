faq:
  - q: "What is the difference between PTK and PRK?"
    a: |
      Both use the same excimer laser on the surface of the cornea. Phototherapeutic keratectomy (PTK) treats disease: it removes scars, deposits and irregularities. Photorefractive keratectomy (PRK) reshapes a healthy cornea to correct a glasses prescription. Topography-guided PTK combines the two ideas, using a detailed map of your cornea to plan a customised treatment that smooths irregularities.
  - q: "Which treatment will I need?"
    a: |
      That depends on the condition, the depth and location of any scar or irregularity, the thickness and strength of your cornea and your visual goals. Your surgeon will map your cornea with topography and tomography and measure it with optical coherence tomography before recommending a plan, which may combine laser with cross-linking, CAIRS or contact lenses.

## How excimer laser treatment works

The excimer laser produces ultraviolet light that removes corneal tissue a fraction of a micrometre at a time without heating the surrounding tissue. Guided by a computer, it can remove a precise depth of tissue in a precise pattern. In therapeutic treatment the pattern is designed to remove diseased tissue or to smooth an irregular surface; in refractive treatment it is designed to change the focusing power of the cornea.

Treatments are performed under anaesthetic eye drops and usually take a few minutes per eye. A soft bandage contact lens protects the eye while the surface heals over the following days.
