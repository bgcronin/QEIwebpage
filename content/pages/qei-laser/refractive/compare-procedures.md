## Corneal or lens-based?

The six procedures fall into two families. **Corneal procedures** (LASIK, CLEAR, TransPRK and PRK) permanently reshape the cornea with a laser. They are quick, well proven, performed on the surface of the eye and best suited to low and moderate prescriptions in younger eyes with normal corneas. **Lens-based procedures** (the EVO ICL and refractive lens exchange) leave the cornea untouched and change the focusing power of the eye with an implant. They suit high prescriptions, thin corneas and, in the case of lens exchange, older eyes that have lost reading focus.

## Within the corneal procedures

- **LASIK** is the fastest to recover from and the most widely performed. Its flap is the reason for its speed and also its main structural compromise.
- **CLEAR** keeps the front of the cornea intact and is gentler on the tear film, at the cost of a slightly slower first few days. It is our usual recommendation for short-sighted people who want to avoid a flap.
- **TransPRK** and **PRK** involve no cutting at all and preserve the most corneal strength, which makes them the safest choice for thinner corneas and for people at risk of eye trauma. They ask for patience: a few days of discomfort and a slower climb to final vision.

## Within the lens procedures

- The **EVO ICL** adds a lens without removing your own, so it preserves natural reading focus in younger eyes and can be reversed. It is the answer for prescriptions beyond laser and for corneas that laser should not touch.
- **Refractive lens exchange** removes the natural lens, so it is reserved for eyes that have already lost reading focus. In return it can correct any prescription, restore reading vision with a trifocal or extended-depth-of-focus implant, and removes the possibility of cataract.

## The three questions your surgeon is answering

1. **Is the cornea safe to reshape?** Tomography, thickness and the amount of tissue the correction would remove decide between laser and a lens-based procedure.
2. **Which procedure gives this eye the best quality of vision?** High prescriptions favour the ICL; ageing lenses favour lens exchange; healthy young corneas favour laser.
3. **Which recovery and risk profile suits this person?** Occupation, sport, dry eye, night driving and personal preference decide between LASIK, CLEAR and surface laser.
