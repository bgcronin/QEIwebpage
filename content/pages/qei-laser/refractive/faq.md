faq:
  - q: "Does laser eye surgery hurt?"
    a: |
      No. Anaesthetic drops numb the surface of the eye completely. During LASIK and CLEAR you feel pressure from the laser interface for about 20 to 30 seconds and your vision dims; during TransPRK you feel nothing. Afterwards the eyes are gritty, watery and light-sensitive for some hours after LASIK, CLEAR and the ICL, and for three to four days after surface laser, which we manage with drops and simple pain relief.
  - q: "Am I awake? What if I blink?"
    a: |
      You are awake but relaxed, with light sedation if you want it. A small spring holds the eyelids gently apart so you cannot blink, and the laser tracks your eye continuously. Your surgeon talks you through every step; most people are surprised how quick and undramatic it is.
  - q: "Are both eyes treated on the same day?"
    a: |
      For LASIK, CLEAR, TransPRK and PRK, yes. Same-day treatment of both eyes is standard worldwide and lets you recover once. For the ICL, both eyes are usually treated the same day or within a few days. For lens exchange, the second eye usually follows one to two weeks after the first.
  - q: "How safe is laser eye surgery?"
    a: |
      Laser vision correction is among the most studied procedures in medicine, with more than 30 years of data and tens of millions of treatments. Sight-threatening complications are rare, in the order of 1 in several thousand, and the risk is lowest when the eye has been screened with corneal tomography and the surgery is performed inside an accredited hospital by a corneal specialist. Every procedure has specific risks, which are set out on each procedure page and discussed with you in person before you consent.
  - q: "Can I go blind?"
    a: |
      Loss of an eye's vision from laser vision correction is extraordinarily rare, and we are not aware of any case of total blindness from modern LASIK, CLEAR or surface laser performed on a properly screened eye. The most serious risks, infection and corneal ectasia, are rare and usually treatable when caught early, which is why we ask you to attend every review and to call us at once if the eye becomes painful or red.
  - q: "How long does the result last?"
    a: |
      Laser reshaping of the cornea is permanent, and the ICL and lens implants are designed to last a lifetime. Your eyes continue to change naturally: reading glasses arrive in the mid-40s regardless of surgery, and a small drift in prescription can occur over decades, particularly in people who were highly short-sighted. Around 2 to 5 per cent of eyes benefit from an enhancement in the first year, which is included in your treatment.
  - q: "What is the right age for laser eye surgery?"
    a: |
      From 18 with a stable prescription, and ideally from 21. There is no upper limit for a healthy eye, but from the mid-40s the needs of the eye change: laser cannot restore reading focus, and from the 50s and 60s refractive lens exchange usually gives a better and longer-lasting result. Your surgeon will recommend the procedure that suits your age as well as your prescription.
  - q: "Can astigmatism be corrected?"
    a: |
      Yes. Every procedure we offer corrects regular astigmatism: laser to about 5 D, the toric ICL to about 4 D and toric lens implants in lens exchange. Irregular astigmatism from keratoconus or scarring is treated through QEI Laser Therapeutic.
  - q: "Will I still need reading glasses?"
    a: |
      If you are under 40, laser or the ICL corrects your distance vision and your natural reading focus is preserved until presbyopia arrives in the mid-40s. If you are already presbyopic, the options are monovision (one eye set slightly nearer, which we trial with contact lenses first) or refractive lens exchange with a trifocal or extended-depth-of-focus implant, which restores reading vision for most people.
  - q: "What about night vision, glare and halos?"
    a: |
      Halos and glare around lights at night are common in the first weeks after any procedure and fade as the eye heals. Modern large treatment zones, ray-traced planning and careful pupil measurement have made persistent night-vision problems uncommon after laser; in the large PROWL studies visual symptoms were more often improved than worsened by LASIK. Multifocal lens implants cause halos that most people adapt to within months.
  - q: "Will laser eye surgery give me dry eyes?"
    a: |
      Temporary dryness is the most common side effect of LASIK, because the flap cuts the corneal nerves that drive tear production, and it usually settles by three to six months. CLEAR and surface laser cut fewer nerves and cause less dryness; the ICL and lens exchange cause very little. If you already have dry eye, we treat it first through our optometrist-led Dry Eye Clinic, and we may recommend a procedure other than LASIK.
  - q: "How soon can I drive, work, exercise and swim?"
    a: |
      After LASIK, the ICL and lens exchange most people drive and return to desk work within one to three days; after CLEAR within two to four days; after TransPRK and PRK within one to two weeks. Light exercise can resume after a few days, swimming after two weeks, and contact sports after four weeks (two weeks for CLEAR and surface laser, which have no flap).
  - q: "Can I wear eye make-up and use screens?"
    a: |
      Screens are fine from the first day, in moderation, with frequent lubricating drops because we blink less when concentrating. Leave eye make-up off for one week and replace mascara and eyeliner, which can harbour bacteria.
  - q: "I am pregnant or breastfeeding. Can I be treated?"
    a: |
      Not yet. Hormones change the prescription and the tear film, and some post-operative drops are best avoided. We are happy to assess you now and schedule surgery three months after breastfeeding ends.
  - q: "What happens if my prescription changes years later?"
    a: |
      Small changes can be corrected with glasses or an enhancement. Larger changes in later life are usually due to the natural lens and are treated with lens exchange or cataract surgery, both of which remain fully possible after laser or the ICL. Keep a record of your treatment, because lens power calculations after laser use special formulas.
  - q: "Can I have laser eye surgery if I have keratoconus, glaucoma or diabetes?"
    a: |
      Keratoconus rules out conventional laser, but cross-linking, CAIRS and topography-guided treatment through QEI Laser Therapeutic can improve vision, and the ICL may be an option in stable eyes. Well-controlled glaucoma and diabetes without eye disease do not exclude you, although the ICL is avoided in glaucoma and we ask for stable diabetic control.
  - q: "Do I need a referral, and is the assessment free?"
    a: |
      No referral is needed. The assessment carries a fee, because it is a complete examination with a specialist surgeon, and that fee is credited against your procedure. A referral from your optometrist or GP allows Medicare to contribute to the consultation.
  - q: "Who will do my surgery?"
    a: |
      One of our four laser surgeons, Dr David Gunn, Dr Brendan Cronin, Dr Lee Holland and Dr Geoffrey Ryan, all Fellows of the Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Ophthalmologists with fellowship training in corneal, cataract and refractive surgery. The surgeon who assesses you performs your procedure and sees you at your reviews.
  - q: "Where is the surgery performed?"

      At South Brisbane Day Hospital, a fully accredited private hospital co-located with the Queensland Eye Institute at 87 Ipswich Road, Woolloongabba, with an anaesthetist on site. Assessments and reviews take place at the QEI Clinic in Woolloongabba or Clayfield.
