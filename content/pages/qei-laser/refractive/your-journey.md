## 1. Get in touch

Call 07 3239 5000 or send an online enquiry. Our team will ask a few questions about your prescription, your eye health and what you hope to achieve, book your assessment and explain how to prepare. No referral is needed, although a referral from your optometrist or GP lets Medicare contribute to the assessment consultation.

## 2. Leave your contact lenses out

Contact lenses mould the cornea and distort the measurements on which your treatment is planned. Leave them out before your assessment and again before surgery:

- soft lenses: at least one week;
- soft toric (astigmatism) lenses: two weeks;
- rigid gas-permeable or orthokeratology lenses: four weeks or longer, until the corneal shape is stable on repeat measurement.

Wear your glasses instead. If you do not have an up-to-date pair, tell us and we will help.

## 3. Your assessment

Allow up to two hours. Your surgeon and our orthoptic and optometric team will measure your prescription, map your corneas in three dimensions, model the optics of the whole eye, examine your tear film, measure your pupils, and dilate your pupils to examine the retina. See [what we measure and why](/qei-laser/refractive/am-i-suitable/).

At the end of the visit your surgeon will tell you which procedures your eyes are suited to, recommend one, and explain the alternatives, the benefits and the risks. You leave with a written quote and consent information to take home. Your pupils will be dilated, so bring sunglasses and do not drive.

## 4. Decide in your own time

There is no pressure to book on the day. Read the consent information, ask questions by phone or email, and involve your optometrist if you wish. Once you decide, our team schedules your surgery, arranges any payment plan and, for the ICL or lens exchange, orders your lenses.

## 5. The week before

- Keep contact lenses out.
- Stop eye make-up, false lashes and lash extensions three days before surgery.
- Collect your prescription drops from the pharmacy and start any drops you have been asked to use beforehand.
- Arrange someone to bring you and take you home, and take the day of surgery and the following day off work.
- If you have been treated for dry eye, keep up your treatment; a healthy surface heals faster.

## 6. The day of surgery

Eat and drink normally unless the anaesthetist has told you otherwise. Wear comfortable clothes and no perfume or scented products, which interfere with the excimer laser. You will be at South Brisbane Day Hospital for one to two hours in total: paperwork and final checks, drops to numb the eye and, if needed, sedation, then the procedure itself, which takes a few minutes per eye. You rest in recovery, are given your drops and instructions, and go home with someone to keep your eyes closed for the afternoon.

## 7. The first 24 hours

Expect grittiness, watering and light sensitivity for several hours, more after surface laser. Sleep is the best medicine. Use the drops as instructed and do not rub your eyes. We see you the next morning to check the eye and, after LASIK, CLEAR, ICL and lens exchange, most people are already seeing well.

## 8. The first week

Return to desk work when you feel ready, usually within one to three days (one to two weeks for surface laser). Wear the shields provided at night for the first week after LASIK. Avoid eye make-up, swimming, saunas, dusty environments and contact sports. Continue your antibiotic and anti-inflammatory drops and use lubricants frequently. Your surgeon will confirm when you meet the driving standard.

## 9. The first three months

Vision continues to sharpen and any night-time halos fade. Drops taper off over two to four weeks, with lubricants continuing longer. Reviews at one month and three months confirm the result is stable. If a small residual prescription remains at three months, your surgeon will discuss an enhancement, which is included in your first year of care.

## 10. Your first year and beyond

A 12-month review completes your treatment package, and we write to your optometrist and GP. After laser you need no special follow-up beyond routine eye examinations every two years. ICL patients have a yearly check of eye pressure, lens position and corneal health. Lens exchange patients are seen if a membrane behind the implant needs a brief laser polish. Your eyes will continue to age like everyone else's, and when reading glasses arrive in the mid-40s, or a cataract much later, we will be here to help.
