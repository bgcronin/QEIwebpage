## How costs are worked out

The cost of vision correction depends on the procedure your eyes are suited to, whether one or both eyes are treated, whether an anaesthetist is needed, and, for lens-based procedures, the lens chosen for you. That is why we do not publish a price list. Instead, after your assessment your surgeon recommends a procedure and you receive a written quote for it, which sets out exactly what is and is not included. The quote is yours to take home, and there is no obligation to book.

## What a quote covers

Your written quote itemises the surgeon's fee, the hospital fee at South Brisbane Day Hospital, the anaesthetist where one is required, any lens or implant, and the post-operative reviews that are part of your care. It also states what is not included, typically prescription eye drops and medicines from the pharmacy, treatment of unrelated eye conditions, and any care beyond the follow-up period. If anything in the quote is unclear, our team will explain it before you decide.

## The assessment

A laser vision assessment is a complete specialist examination with the surgeon who would perform your treatment, and it carries a consultation fee. No referral is needed, but with a referral from your optometrist or GP Medicare contributes to the consultation and tests. See [what the assessment involves](/qei-laser/refractive/am-i-suitable/).

## Medicare, health funds and tax

**Medicare** provides no rebate for vision correction surgery on a healthy eye, but it does contribute to your assessment consultation and tests when you attend with a referral. Where the operation treats disease, for example cataract surgery or therapeutic laser for corneal scarring, Medicare and hospital cover apply; see [QEI Laser Therapeutic](/qei-laser/therapeutic/health-fund-rebates/).

**Private health funds** vary. A minority of funds contribute towards laser eye surgery from their highest extras tiers, often with a waiting period and a lifetime limit; most policies contribute nothing. Check with your fund before your assessment and ask for the exact benefit in writing.

**Tax and superannuation.** Vision correction is a private expense: it is not tax deductible and early release of superannuation is generally not available for it.

## Payment

Our team will explain the payment arrangements, including when payment is due and the options for spreading the cost, when you receive your quote. You are welcome to ask about costs at any point, from your first phone call onwards.

## Thinking about value

Glasses and contact lenses are a lifelong expense that most people never add up, and for many patients the practical value of vision correction matters more than the arithmetic: waking to a clear room, sport and travel without lenses, and, for lens exchange patients, the end of reading glasses. Your surgeon will be honest about what each procedure can and cannot deliver so that you can weigh that value for yourself.
