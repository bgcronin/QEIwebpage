## Current clinical trials at QEI

The Queensland Eye Institute is participating in the following clinical trials. Contact the QEI Clinical Trial Unit on **07 3239 5001** or <a href="mailto:clinical.trials@qei.org.au">clinical.trials@qei.org.au</a> for information about eligibility.

### Geographic atrophy

**Tinlarebant (PHOENIX):** a phase 3, randomised, placebo-controlled study of tinlarebant to explore safety and efficacy in geographic atrophy. [Read more on ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05949593).

### Neovascular age-related macular degeneration

**Velodrome (WR42221):** a phase IIIb, global, multi-centre, randomised, visual assessor-masked study of the efficacy, safety and pharmacokinetics of a 36-week refill regimen for the port delivery system with ranibizumab in patients with neovascular age-related macular degeneration. [Read more on ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04657289).

Our ophthalmologists also participate in trials for other conditions, including thyroid eye disease and inherited retinal disease, as they become available. [Register your interest](https://qei.org.au/clinical-trials-contact-registration/) to be contacted about future trials.

## Why clinical trials matter

Clinical trials confirm whether new medicines and treatments are safe and effective before they are introduced as treatments for a particular disease or condition. The Queensland Eye Institute is committed to supporting initiatives that improve health outcomes for patients with eye disease, including improved access to new drugs that may be more effective than those currently available.

Each study is designed to answer specific questions and find better ways to screen, diagnose, prevent or treat a disease. Some investigations look at how people respond to new treatments; others compare existing medications, test new ways to combine them or observe how people respond to other factors that might affect their health.

## How trials work

Clinical trials are typically conducted in phases:

- **Phase 1** involves a small number of participants, testing a range of doses and analysing any side effects.
- **Phase 2** involves a larger group and a more detailed evaluation of safety and effectiveness.
- **Phase 3** involves a much larger group in which the experimental treatment is formally compared with the current standard treatment, allocated randomly to avoid bias. Trials may be blinded, so neither you nor the trial staff know which treatment you receive, and some compare a treatment with a placebo. You will be told if the trial is blinded or placebo-controlled.

Trials can involve people of all ages and all stages of a condition. All trials have guidelines, called inclusion and exclusion criteria, that determine who can take part, so that results are reliable and participants are safe.

## Informed consent

Before you take part, a QEI doctor will explain the trial: what is involved, its purpose, the tests and procedures, and the risks and benefits. You receive a written participant information and consent form and can take your time, ask questions and discuss it with family or your own doctor before deciding. Signing the form means you understand and agree to take part; you remain free to withdraw at any time. Participants are also informed of new developments throughout the trial.

## Benefits and considerations

People choose to take part because a trial may play an active role in their care, give early access to new treatments, provide focused attention and monitoring, increase their knowledge of eye disease, and help others by contributing to medical research. Potential downsides include side effects, extra time commitments and the possibility that the treatment is not effective for you. Discuss these with your doctor before deciding.

## How to take part

1. Read the trial information and take it to your optometrist, GP or ophthalmologist to confirm you meet the eligibility criteria.
2. Ask them to refer you to the QEI clinical trials department.
3. Call **07 3239 5000** to let us know you have a referral and are interested in more information.

Our multidisciplinary trials team of ophthalmologists, optometrists, orthoptists and clinical assistants will guide you through screening and, if eligible, the trial itself.

## Your rights and feedback

The Australian Charter of Healthcare Rights applies to everyone participating in research. We value feedback from all trial participants: complete our [anonymous survey](https://qei.org.au/clinical-trials/feedback-on-clinical-trials/) at any time.
