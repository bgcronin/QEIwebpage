faq:
  - q: "Why do my eyes water if they are dry?"
    a: "Watery eyes are one of the most common signs of dry eye. When the tear film is unstable the surface of the eye becomes irritated and triggers a flood of reflex tears, which are watery and do not contain the oil the eye needs, so they run down the cheek without helping."
  - q: "Is dry eye caused by screens?"
    a: "Screens do not cause dry eye disease on their own, but we blink far less when concentrating on a screen, which lets the tears evaporate and leaves oil sitting in the glands. For people with early gland dysfunction this can tip them into symptoms. Regular blink breaks help, and treating the glands helps far more."
  - q: "Can dry eye be cured?"
    a: "Dry eye is usually a chronic condition that is controlled rather than cured, in the same way as asthma or eczema. With the right diagnosis and treatment most people gain lasting relief and need only maintenance care."
  - q: "Why don't my drops work?"
    a: "Lubricating drops replace moisture but do not treat blocked glands, eyelid inflammation or mites, which are the usual underlying causes. Drops containing preservatives can also irritate the eye when used frequently. A dry eye assessment identifies what is really driving your symptoms."
  - q: "Does menopause cause dry eye?"
    a: "Hormonal changes around menopause affect the oil glands and tear production, and dry eye becomes considerably more common in women from midlife. The same treatments are effective."
  - q: "I have had laser eye surgery. Is my dry eye permanent?"
    a: "Dry eye is common in the months after laser vision correction and usually improves as the corneal nerves recover. Persistent symptoms are treatable, and the Dry Eye Clinic works closely with QEI's refractive surgeons."
  - q: "Is IPL only for dry eye?"
    a: "In the Dry Eye Clinic, IPL is used specifically for meibomian gland dysfunction and its associated conditions such as ocular rosacea and Demodex. It is a medical treatment performed with an ophthalmic device, not a cosmetic procedure."
  - q: "Are there conditions that make dry eye worse?"
    a: "Yes. Autoimmune diseases such as Sjögren's syndrome, rheumatoid arthritis and thyroid disease, skin conditions such as rosacea, diabetes, and medications including antihistamines, some antidepressants, isotretinoin and some blood pressure tablets all contribute. Tell your optometrist about your general health so these can be addressed."
  - q: "Do children get dry eye?"
    a: "Children and teenagers can develop blepharitis, styes and dry eye, particularly with allergies, heavy screen use or contact lens wear. Painless treatments such as low level light therapy and eyelid cleaning are well suited to younger patients."
  - q: "When is dry eye an emergency?"
    a: "Dry eye itself is not an emergency, but sudden severe pain, marked redness, light sensitivity, sudden loss of vision or a red eye in a contact lens wearer need same-day care. Call 07 3239 5000 during clinic hours or see our emergency eye care advice."
