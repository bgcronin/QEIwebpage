## Why Demodex matters for dry eye

Two species of Demodex mite live in and around eyelash follicles and the meibomian glands. When their numbers grow they block follicles, damage the eyelid margin and glands, carry bacteria and trigger inflammation. The result is chronic blepharitis, an unstable tear film and dry eye symptoms that never quite settle with drops.

## How we treat Demodex

- **In-clinic eyelid cleaning.** [BlephEx](/dry-eye-clinic/treatments/blephex/) exfoliates the lid margins and lash bases, removing mites, eggs and the debris they live in.
- **Light-based treatment.** [Intense pulsed light](/dry-eye-clinic/treatments/ipl-optilight/) has been shown to reduce Demodex numbers while treating the associated meibomian gland dysfunction.
- **Home programme.** Daily lid treatment with a tea tree oil based lid cleanser or foam over six to eight weeks targets the mites' life cycle. Your optometrist will recommend a product and show you how to use it safely.
- **Prescription options.** Newer prescription treatments for Demodex blepharitis are becoming available, and our optometrists can advise whether one is suitable for you.
- **Review.** Lashes are re-examined at follow-up to confirm the mites have cleared and to plan maintenance.
