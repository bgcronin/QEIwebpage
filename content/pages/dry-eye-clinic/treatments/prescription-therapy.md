## Treating the inflammation behind dry eye

When the tear film is unstable, the surface of the eye becomes inflamed, and inflammation in turn damages the glands and cells that make tears. Breaking this cycle is essential for people with moderate to severe dry eye, and lubricating drops alone cannot do it.

## What we can prescribe and recommend

- **Cyclosporine eye drops** are the mainstay of long-term anti-inflammatory treatment. They are used every day, take several weeks to reach full effect and are continued for months.
- **Short courses of anti-inflammatory drops** to settle a flare quickly while longer-term treatment takes effect, with careful monitoring.
- **Preservative-free lubricants and gels** chosen to match your tear film, because preservatives in many over-the-counter drops can worsen inflammation.
- **Nutritional support** such as omega-3 fatty acids, which have an anti-inflammatory effect on the meibomian glands.
- **Management of contributing factors** including allergy, contact lens wear, medications and environmental triggers.

Where treatment falls outside optometry prescribing, for example oral antibiotics for rosacea-related gland disease, immune-modulating therapy or autologous serum drops, we arrange it with your GP or a QEI ophthalmologist so your care stays joined up.

## Monitoring your progress

Anti-inflammatory therapy works best alongside the procedures offered in the clinic, such as [IPL](/dry-eye-clinic/treatments/ipl-optilight/), [QMR](/dry-eye-clinic/treatments/qmr-rexon-eye/) and [gland expression](/dry-eye-clinic/treatments/meibomian-gland-expression/). Your optometrist reviews your symptoms and tear film measurements at each visit and adjusts your medicines accordingly.
