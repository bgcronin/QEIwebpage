## How urgent referrals work

Call **07 3239 5000** during clinic hours (Monday to Friday) and tell reception the referral is urgent. A member of our clinical team will discuss the case, arrange a same-day or next-available slot with the appropriate subspecialist, and advise on interim management. Please then email the referral to <a href="mailto:reception@qei.org.au">reception@qei.org.au</a> marked **URGENT**.

Outside clinic hours, please direct patients to a hospital emergency department, which can access the on-call ophthalmology service.

## Presentations needing same-day assessment

- Sudden painless loss of vision, including suspected retinal artery or vein occlusion.
- Symptoms of retinal tear or detachment: new flashes, a shower of floaters, a field defect or curtain.
- Suspected acute angle-closure glaucoma: severe pain, halos, nausea, hazy cornea, mid-dilated pupil.
- Suspected infective keratitis or corneal ulcer, particularly in contact lens wearers.
- Post-operative endophthalmitis: pain, redness and reduced vision after intraocular surgery or injection.
- Penetrating or blunt trauma with reduced vision, hyphaema or lid laceration involving the margin.
- Chemical injury (after immediate irrigation).
- Orbital cellulitis or a rapidly progressive orbital process.
- Suspected giant cell arteritis with visual symptoms.
- New third nerve palsy with pupil involvement, or new papilloedema.
- Acute anterior uveitis with significant pain or reduced vision.

## Presentations needing priority (within one to two weeks)

- New distortion or central blur suggesting neovascular age-related macular degeneration.
- New onset diplopia without red flags.
- Optic neuritis or unexplained optic disc swelling.
- Significant intraocular pressure elevation without acute symptoms.
- Rapid progression of keratoconus in young patients.
- Diabetic retinopathy with suspected macular oedema or proliferative change.
- Thyroid eye disease with active inflammation, diplopia or concern about optic nerve function.

When in doubt, phone. We would rather discuss a case than have a patient wait.
