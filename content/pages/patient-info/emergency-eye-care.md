## Call 000 immediately for

- A **chemical splash** to the eye. Start rinsing the eye with clean water or saline straight away and keep rinsing for at least 15 to 20 minutes while help is arranged. Do not wait to see if it improves.
- A **penetrating injury** or an object embedded in the eye. Do not remove it, do not press on the eye, and shield it loosely until help arrives.
- **Sudden loss of vision together with** weakness, numbness, facial droop, confusion or difficulty speaking, which may be a stroke.

## Seek same-day care for

- **Sudden loss or blurring of vision** in one or both eyes, even if painless.
- A **curtain, shadow or veil** across part of your vision.
- **Flashes of light with a sudden shower of new floaters.**
- **Severe eye pain**, especially with nausea, headache, halos around lights or a hazy cornea.
- A **painful red eye with light sensitivity**, or any red eye in a **contact lens wearer**.
- **Sudden double vision** or a **drooping eyelid** that is new.
- **Increasing pain, redness or reduced vision after eye surgery or an injection**.
- A **blow to the eye** with reduced vision, blood visible inside the eye, or a cut eyelid.

## Where to go

- **During clinic hours (Monday to Friday):** call QEI on **[07 3239 5000](tel:+61732395000)** and tell reception the problem is urgent. We triage urgent problems the same day and can direct you to the right specialist. Patients recovering from surgery should follow the after-hours instructions provided by their surgeon.
- **Outside clinic hours:** go to your nearest hospital emergency department. Public hospitals can call the on-call ophthalmology service.
- **Not sure?** Call **13 HEALTH (13 43 25 84)**, Queensland Health's 24-hour advice line, or your GP.

## While you wait

- Do not drive yourself if your vision is affected. Ask someone to take you.
- Do not rub or press on the eye.
- Do not use eye drops other than saline or water unless told to by a health professional.
- If you wear contact lenses, remove them and bring them and the case with you.
- Bring your medicines list and any eye drops you use.

## Eye problems that are usually not emergencies

Gritty, tired or watery eyes, mild redness without pain, a small stye, or a gradual change in your glasses prescription are not usually urgent. Book a routine appointment or, for dry eye symptoms, contact our [Dry Eye Clinic](/dry-eye-clinic/). If in doubt, call us: it is always better to check.
