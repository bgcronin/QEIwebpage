## What is glaucoma?

Glaucoma is a group of conditions in which the optic nerve, which carries visual information from the eye to the brain, is progressively damaged. The damage is usually related to the pressure inside the eye, although it can occur at normal pressures. Because glaucoma first affects peripheral vision and the brain fills in the gaps, people are often unaware of the loss until it is advanced. Glaucoma is a silent disease with no clear symptoms in its early stages; often people do not know they have it until they have lost significant vision.

The main types are **primary open-angle glaucoma** (the most common, slow and painless), **angle-closure glaucoma** (drainage is blocked by the iris; it can be gradual or acute), **normal-tension glaucoma** and **secondary glaucoma** caused by other conditions, injury or medications.

## Symptoms

- Usually none in the early stages.
- Gradual loss of peripheral (side) vision, progressing to tunnel vision.
- In acute angle-closure: sudden severe eye pain, headache, nausea, red eye, blurred vision and halos around lights. This is an emergency.

## Causes and risk factors

- Raised eye pressure, though glaucoma can occur with normal pressure.
- Age over 50, and a family history of glaucoma (first-degree relatives have a much higher risk).
- Short-sightedness (open-angle) or long-sightedness (angle-closure).
- Thin corneas, previous eye injury, long-term steroid use, diabetes, migraine and low blood pressure.
- African, Asian or Hispanic ancestry increases the risk of particular types.

## Diagnosis

A glaucoma assessment includes measurement of eye pressure, examination of the drainage angle (gonioscopy), assessment of the optic nerve, OCT scans of the nerve fibre layer and visual field testing. Corneal thickness is measured because it affects pressure readings. Comparing these results over time shows whether the disease is stable or progressing.

## Treatment at QEI

Treatment aims to lower eye pressure to a level that prevents further damage:

- **Eye drops** taken every day are the most common first treatment.
- **Laser.** Selective laser trabeculoplasty (SLT) for open-angle glaucoma and laser iridotomy for angle-closure are quick clinic procedures.
- **Minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS)** uses tiny stents or devices, often combined with cataract surgery, to improve drainage with a fast recovery.
- **Drainage surgery.** Trabeculectomy and glaucoma tube (drainage device) surgery are used for advanced or difficult-to-control glaucoma. QEI's glaucoma surgeons were the first in Australia to implant newer tube devices.

QEI's glaucoma specialists have subspecialty fellowship training from leading international centres and offer the full range of medical, laser and surgical options.

## Living with glaucoma

Use your drops exactly as prescribed, attend every review even when your vision seems unchanged, and tell your family to have their eyes checked. Glaucoma is far easier to control than to catch up with.
