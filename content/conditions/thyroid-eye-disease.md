## What is thyroid eye disease?

Thyroid eye disease (TED) or Graves' ophthalmopathy is a condition in which the fatty tissue around and behind the eye, and the muscles that move the eye, become inflamed and swollen. Because the eye sits in a bony socket, the swelling pushes the eye forward and can restrict its movement. The condition is autoimmune and is most often associated with Graves' disease, an overactive thyroid, although it can occur with an underactive or normal thyroid.

TED typically has an **active phase**, in which inflammation causes symptoms to change and progress over months to a few years, followed by an **inactive phase** in which inflammation settles but some changes may remain.

## Symptoms

- Grittiness, dryness, watering and redness of the eyes.
- Swelling of the eyelids and tissues around the eyes.
- Bulging or staring appearance (proptosis) and retraction of the eyelids.
- Aching or pressure behind the eyes, worse with eye movement.
- Double vision, particularly looking up or to the side.
- Rarely, blurred or dim vision and loss of colour vision, which can indicate pressure on the optic nerve and needs urgent care.

## Causes and risk factors

The immune system targets tissues in the eye socket that share features with the thyroid gland. Risk is higher in people with Graves' disease, particularly women, and **smoking** is the strongest modifiable risk factor, making the disease more severe and treatment less effective. Radioactive iodine treatment of the thyroid can worsen eye disease in some people, and unstable thyroid hormone levels also contribute.

## Diagnosis

Diagnosis is based on the clinical examination, including measurements of eye protrusion, eyelid position, eye movements and vision, together with thyroid blood tests. Imaging of the eye sockets (CT or MRI) shows enlargement of the muscles and fat, and vision, colour vision and visual field tests monitor the optic nerve. Scoring of clinical activity helps determine whether the disease is in the active or inactive phase, which guides treatment.

## Treatment at QEI

Care is coordinated between your endocrinologist, who manages the thyroid, and QEI's oculoplastic and orbital specialist, Dr Alexandra Manta, who also leads research into thyroid eye disease.

- **Supportive measures** for mild disease: lubricating drops and gels, sleeping with the head raised, sunglasses, selenium supplements and, above all, stopping smoking.
- **Medical treatment** of active moderate to severe disease, including corticosteroids, immune-modulating medicines and newer targeted therapies that block the receptor driving the inflammation. Orbital radiotherapy is used in selected cases.
- **Clinical trials.** QEI participates in trials of new treatments for thyroid eye disease; you may be eligible to take part.
- **Surgery** once the disease is stable: orbital decompression to create space and reduce bulging, eye muscle surgery for double vision, and eyelid surgery to correct retraction and improve closure and appearance. Urgent decompression is performed if the optic nerve is threatened.

## Living with TED

Regular reviews during the active phase allow treatment to be adjusted quickly. Keep thyroid levels stable with your endocrinologist, do not smoke, and report any change in vision immediately.
