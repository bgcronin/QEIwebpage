## What is dry eye?

The human eye is the most amazing camera found anywhere in nature, but like all good cameras it has to be kept clean. Our tears play a vital role in keeping the surface of the eye free from debris, and in keeping our eyes white, comfortable and seeing clearly.

Tears are a complex structure with three layers: an outer **oil** layer that slows evaporation, a **water** layer that hydrates and nourishes, and an inner **mucin** layer that helps tears spread evenly. These layers must be in balance and work in harmony. Dry eye disease occurs when there is not enough tear, the tears evaporate too quickly, or both, and the surface of the eye becomes inflamed.

The oil layer is made by the meibomian glands in the eyelids. When these glands do not work properly, a condition called **meibomian gland dysfunction** or blepharitis, the tears evaporate quickly. This is the most common cause of dry eye.

## Symptoms

- Gritty, sandy, burning or stinging eyes.
- Watery eyes, particularly outdoors, in wind or in air-conditioning.
- Vision that fluctuates and clears with blinking.
- Tired, heavy eyes after reading or screen use.
- Redness, crusty lashes, recurrent styes.
- Discomfort with contact lenses.

## Causes and risk factors

There are many causes of dry eye, including:

- Age and hormonal changes, particularly menopause.
- Meibomian gland dysfunction, blepharitis and Demodex mites.
- Allergies and skin conditions such as rosacea.
- Medications, including antihistamines, some antidepressants and blood pressure medicines, and isotretinoin.
- Environmental factors: air-conditioning, wind, low humidity and prolonged screen use with reduced blinking.
- Autoimmune conditions such as Sjögren's syndrome, rheumatoid arthritis and thyroid disease.
- Contact lens wear and previous eye surgery, including laser vision correction.

## Diagnosis

A dry eye assessment includes a symptom questionnaire and examination of the tear film, the surface of the eye and the eyelids under magnification. Tests measure how quickly the tears break up, look for damage to the surface, and assess the structure and function of the meibomian glands, so the type of dry eye and its contributing factors can be identified.
