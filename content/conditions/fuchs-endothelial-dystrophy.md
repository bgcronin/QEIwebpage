## What is Fuchs' endothelial dystrophy?

The cornea is the clear, dome-shaped window on the front of the eye. Its inner surface is lined by a single layer of endothelial cells that constantly pump fluid out of the cornea to keep it thin and transparent. These cells do not regenerate. In Fuchs' endothelial dystrophy the cells are lost faster than normal and develop small bumps called guttae. As the pump fails, fluid accumulates, the cornea swells and vision becomes hazy.

## Symptoms

- Blurred or foggy vision that is worst on waking and clears over the morning, because fluid builds up overnight while the eyes are closed.
- Glare and halos around lights.
- As the condition progresses, blur that no longer clears during the day.
- In advanced disease, painful blisters (bullae) on the corneal surface.

## Causes and risk factors

Fuchs' dystrophy is genetic and often runs in families, and it is more common in women. Symptoms usually appear after the age of 50, although the guttae can be seen decades earlier. Eye surgery, particularly cataract surgery, can accelerate cell loss in a cornea that is already compromised.

## Diagnosis

Your ophthalmologist can see guttae and swelling with a slit-lamp examination. Specular microscopy counts the endothelial cells, and pachymetry and anterior segment OCT measure corneal thickness, allowing changes to be tracked over time.

## Treatment at QEI

In the early stages, hypertonic saline drops or ointment can draw fluid from the cornea and improve morning blur, and a hair dryer held at arm's length can speed clearing. Regular monitoring is all that many people need.

When swelling affects vision or comfort, corneal transplantation is recommended. QEI's corneal surgeons perform **Descemet membrane endothelial keratoplasty (DMEK)**, which replaces only the failed inner layer with a thin sheet of donor cells through a small incision. Compared with older full-thickness transplants, DMEK offers faster visual recovery, a lower risk of rejection and a stronger eye. DSAEK, a related technique, is used in selected cases. Where a cataract is also present, cataract surgery and DMEK may be combined.

## After surgery

You will lie on your back for a period after DMEK to keep the graft in place, use drops for several months, and attend regular reviews. Most people notice clearer vision within weeks. Lifelong follow-up ensures the graft stays healthy.
