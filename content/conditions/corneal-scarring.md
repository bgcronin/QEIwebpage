## What is corneal scarring?

The cornea is the clear window at the front of the eye. To focus light effectively it must be perfectly clear and regular in shape. When the cornea is damaged deeply enough, it heals with scar tissue that is not transparent. A scar scatters and blocks light, and if it distorts the corneal surface it causes irregular astigmatism that glasses cannot correct.

## Symptoms

- Blurred or hazy vision, worst when the scar lies over the pupil.
- Glare and haloes around lights, especially at night.
- Light sensitivity and, for some people, recurrent discomfort or watering.
- A visible white or grey mark on the cornea.

## Causes and risk factors

- **Infection.** Bacterial, viral (herpes simplex or shingles), fungal or amoebic keratitis, often related to contact lens wear, injury or a weakened surface.
- **Injury.** Scratches, foreign bodies, chemical burns and blunt trauma.
- **Disease.** Advanced keratoconus (including hydrops), severe dry eye and ocular surface disease, corneal dystrophies and previous surgery.

## Diagnosis

Your ophthalmologist examines the cornea with a slit lamp and maps its shape and thickness with corneal topography or tomography. Anterior segment OCT shows the depth of the scar, which determines the treatment options. Vision is checked with a rigid contact lens trial to see how much improvement is possible.

## Treatment at QEI

Treatment is matched to the depth and position of the scar:

- **Glasses and contact lenses.** Rigid gas-permeable or scleral contact lenses vault over the irregular surface and can dramatically improve vision for many people, with no surgery needed.
- **Laser treatment.** Superficial scars can be polished away with phototherapeutic keratectomy (PTK), an excimer laser procedure.
- **Corneal transplantation.** Deeper scars may need replacement of the affected corneal layers (DALK) or the full-thickness cornea (penetrating keratoplasty). QEI's corneal surgeons have fellowship training from leading international centres and perform all types of corneal transplant.
- **Treating the cause.** Managing active infection, dry eye or inflammation is essential to prevent further scarring, and our [Dry Eye Clinic](/dry-eye-clinic/) supports patients with ocular surface disease.

## Preventing corneal scars

Good contact lens hygiene, never sleeping or swimming in lenses, protective eyewear for hazardous work and prompt treatment of any painful red eye all reduce the risk of the infections and injuries that cause scarring.
