## What are retinal tears and detachment?

The retina is a thin layer of nerve tissue that lines the inside of the eyeball. It senses light, similar to the sensor in a digital camera, and sends images to the brain. The centre of the eye is filled with a clear gel, the vitreous, which is attached to the retina. As we age the vitreous shrinks and separates from the retina, a normal event called posterior vitreous detachment. Occasionally it pulls hard enough to tear the retina.

Fluid can then pass through the tear and lift the retina away from the wall of the eye, a **retinal detachment**. A detached retina cannot work properly and, without treatment, leads to permanent loss of vision in that eye.

## Symptoms

- A sudden increase in floaters: spots, cobwebs or a swarm of dots drifting in your vision.
- Flashes of light like lightning streaks, especially in the outer vision and in dim light.
- A shadow, grey curtain or veil spreading across part of your vision.
- Blurred or reduced vision, sometimes progressing over hours or days.

A retinal tear and early detachment are painless, so symptoms alone should prompt urgent assessment.

## Causes and risk factors

- Age-related vitreous separation (most common after 50).
- Short-sightedness (myopia), particularly high myopia.
- Previous cataract or other eye surgery, or eye injury.
- A previous retinal detachment in the other eye, or a family history.
- Thin areas of retina such as lattice degeneration, and some inherited conditions.

## Diagnosis

An ophthalmologist examines the entire retina through dilated pupils, sometimes gently indenting the eye to see the far edges. Ultrasound is used if the view is obscured by blood, and OCT shows whether the macula is involved. Same-day assessment is arranged for anyone with new flashes and floaters.

## Treatment at QEI

- **Retinal tear** without detachment is sealed with laser (retinopexy) or freezing treatment (cryotherapy) in the clinic, creating a scar that stops fluid passing through the tear. This takes minutes and prevents most detachments.
- **Retinal detachment** needs surgery by a vitreoretinal surgeon. Options include **vitrectomy** (removing the vitreous gel, flattening the retina and filling the eye with gas or oil), a **scleral buckle** (a silicone band around the eye to support the retina) and, in selected cases, **pneumatic retinopexy** (a gas bubble injected in the clinic). QEI's vitreoretinal surgeons, Associate Professor Anthony Kwan and Associate Professor Abhishek Sharma, operate at South Brisbane Day Hospital and in the public system.

## Recovery and outlook

Most detachments are successfully reattached with one operation, and additional surgery is sometimes needed. Recovery of vision depends on how much of the retina was detached and for how long, particularly whether the macula was involved, which is why acting on symptoms within hours matters. After surgery you may need to maintain a head position, avoid flying while gas is in the eye, and attend close follow-up.
