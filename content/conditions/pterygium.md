## What is a pterygium?

A pterygium (the plural is pterygia) is a wedge-shaped, fleshy growth of the conjunctiva, the thin membrane covering the white of the eye, that extends onto the cornea. It usually appears on the side of the eye nearest the nose. It is not a cancer, but as it grows across the cornea it can pull the cornea out of shape, causing astigmatism, and eventually block vision if it reaches the pupil.

Australia has one of the highest rates of pterygia in the world due to our sunny climate, outdoor lifestyle and long hours of ultraviolet exposure, which is why it is sometimes called surfer's eye.

## Symptoms

- A visible pink or red growth on the white of the eye, spreading onto the cornea.
- Redness, irritation, grittiness or a feeling that something is in the eye.
- Dryness and watering.
- Blurred vision from astigmatism as the growth advances.
- Difficulty wearing contact lenses.

## Causes and risk factors

Long-term exposure to ultraviolet light is the main cause, along with wind, dust and dry conditions. People who work or play outdoors, live in sunny climates, and those who spent their childhood outdoors without eye protection are most at risk. A pinguecula, a small yellowish bump on the conjunctiva, is an earlier sun-related change that may precede a pterygium.

## Diagnosis

A pterygium is diagnosed by examination with a slit lamp. Photographs and corneal topography document its size and effect on the cornea so growth can be tracked. Rarely, other growths on the eye can look similar, so any unusual lesion should be examined by an ophthalmologist.

## Treatment at QEI

For small, stable pterygia, lubricating drops, wraparound sunglasses and a broad-brimmed hat reduce symptoms and slow growth. Short courses of anti-inflammatory drops can settle flare-ups.

When surgery is needed, QEI's corneal surgeons use modern techniques in which the pterygium is carefully excised and the bare area is covered with a graft of the patient's own conjunctiva, secured with tissue adhesive rather than sutures. This **sutureless approach** is more comfortable, heals faster and has a very low rate of recurrence. Surgery is a day procedure under local anaesthetic at South Brisbane Day Hospital.

## Prevention

Wear close-fitting or wraparound sunglasses that block UV, and a hat, whenever you are outdoors, and protect children's eyes from an early age. Sun protection is also important after surgery to reduce the risk of recurrence.
