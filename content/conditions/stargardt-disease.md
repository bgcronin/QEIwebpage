## What is Stargardt disease?

Stargardt disease is a rare genetic condition that affects the macula, the part of the retina responsible for sharp central vision. In most cases it is caused by changes in a gene called ABCA4, which affects how the retina processes vitamin A. A waste product called lipofuscin builds up in and beneath the light-sensing cells of the macula, damaging them over time.

Vision loss usually begins between the ages of about six and twenty, although it can start later, and progresses at different rates in different people. Peripheral vision is usually preserved, so people with Stargardt disease remain mobile and independent, but reading, recognising faces and driving become difficult.

## Symptoms

- Gradual loss of central vision in both eyes, often first noticed at school or when reading.
- Blurred, distorted or grey patches in the centre of vision.
- Difficulty adapting from bright to dim light, and sensitivity to glare.
- Reduced colour vision in later stages.

## Diagnosis

Stargardt disease can be missed in its early stages because the retina may look normal. Diagnosis at QEI uses a combination of detailed retinal imaging, including OCT and fundus autofluorescence, which shows the characteristic flecks and the pattern of damage, electrodiagnostic tests such as the electroretinogram provided through the Queensland Electro-Diagnostic and Imaging Centre, and **genetic testing**, which confirms the diagnosis and informs eligibility for trials and family counselling.

## Management and support

Although there is no cure yet, a great deal can be done:

- **Protecting the retina.** Avoiding high-dose vitamin A supplements and wearing UV-blocking sunglasses and a hat are generally recommended.
- **Low-vision support.** Magnifiers, high-contrast and large-print materials, screen readers, lighting and orientation training help people study, work and live independently. We can refer you to low-vision and support services.
- **Genetic counselling** for you and your family.
- **Regular monitoring** to track the condition and detect any treatable problems.
- **Clinical trials.** QEI participates in research on inherited retinal diseases and in clinical trials for retinal degenerations. [Register your interest](/clinical-trials/) to be contacted about suitable trials.
