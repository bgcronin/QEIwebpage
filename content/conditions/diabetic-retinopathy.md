## What is diabetic retinopathy?

Diabetes mellitus is a significant disease with potentially devastating effects on the eye. Over time, high blood glucose damages the tiny blood vessels that supply the retina. Vessels leak fluid and blood, close off and starve the retina of oxygen, and in advanced disease new abnormal vessels grow that bleed and scar. Fluid collecting in the macula, called **diabetic macular oedema**, is the most common cause of vision loss in people with diabetes.

Retinopathy is described as **non-proliferative** (early, with leaking and blocked vessels) or **proliferative** (advanced, with new vessel growth). Both forms can occur with or without macular oedema.

## Symptoms

Early diabetic retinopathy causes no symptoms. Later signs include:

- Blurred, fluctuating or distorted central vision.
- Dark spots, floaters or cobwebs, which may indicate bleeding.
- Difficulty reading and seeing colours.
- Sudden loss of vision from a large haemorrhage or retinal detachment.

## Causes and risk factors

The longer you have had diabetes and the higher your average blood glucose, the greater the risk. High blood pressure, high cholesterol, kidney disease, smoking and pregnancy increase the risk and speed of progression. Type 1 and type 2 diabetes both cause retinopathy.

## Diagnosis

A dilated examination of the retina, retinal photography and OCT scans detect changes long before symptoms appear. OCT measures macular thickening precisely, and fluorescein or OCT angiography maps leaking and closed vessels when treatment is being planned. Many people with diabetes are screened by their optometrist and referred to QEI when treatment or specialist monitoring is needed.

## Treatment at QEI

- **Managing diabetes.** Good control of blood glucose, blood pressure and cholesterol, with your GP, endocrinologist or diabetes educator, is the foundation of all treatment.
- **Anti-VEGF injections** into the eye are the main treatment for diabetic macular oedema and are also used for proliferative retinopathy. They reduce leakage and abnormal vessel growth and can improve vision.
- **Laser treatment.** Pan-retinal photocoagulation treats proliferative retinopathy, and focal laser is sometimes used for macular oedema.
- **Steroid implants** may be used for macular oedema in selected patients.
- **Vitrectomy surgery** by our vitreoretinal surgeons removes blood from the eye and repairs traction on the retina in advanced disease.

## Protecting your sight

Keep to your recommended eye examination schedule even when your vision seems fine, share your results with your diabetes care team, do not smoke, and report any new floaters or blur promptly. Treatment is far more effective when started early.
